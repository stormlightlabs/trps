The parser reads a file once and keeps the whole text in memory. Byte offsets
point back into that buffer, so a finding can quote the exact slice it matched
without a second pass.

Each detector runs over the same buffer. Phrase matching uses one Aho-Corasick
automaton built at startup, and the other detectors walk the text directly.
Results are sorted by start offset before they reach the printer.

Adding a rule means adding a TOML entry or a small function. Both paths end at
the same finding type, which keeps the printer unaware of where a result came
from.
